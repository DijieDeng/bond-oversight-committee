## 4. Detailed Trial Descriptions

### 4.1 REPRIEVE — Pitavastatin to Reduce Cardiovascular Disease in HIV

| Field | Details |
|---|---|
| **NCT ID** | NCT02344290 |
| **Official Title** | Randomized Trial to Prevent Vascular Events in HIV (REPRIEVE) |
| **Brief Title** | Evaluating the Use of Pitavastatin to Reduce the Risk of Cardiovascular Disease in HIV-Infected Adults |
| **Status** | COMPLETED |
| **Phase** | Phase 3 |
| **Lead Sponsor** | National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID) — NIH |
| **Collaborators** | NHLBI (NIH), Kowa Pharmaceuticals, Gilead Sciences, Massachusetts General Hospital, NEAT ID Foundation |
| **Study Type** | Interventional — Randomized, Parallel, Quadruple-Blind |
| **Primary Purpose** | Prevention |
| **Conditions** | HIV, Cardiovascular Diseases |
| **Enrollment** | 7,769 (actual) |
| **Start Date** | March 26, 2015 |
| **Completion Date** | August 21, 2023 |
| **Illinois Sites** | Northwestern University CRS (Chicago), Rush University CRS (Chicago), UIC Project Wish CRS (Chicago) |

**Summary:** This landmark trial evaluated whether pitavastatin (a statin medication) could reduce the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in adults living with HIV who were on antiretroviral therapy. The trial enrolled 7,769 participants across approximately 120 sites in 11 countries. The Data and Safety Monitoring Board recommended stopping the trial for efficacy in March 2023, concluding that pitavastatin significantly reduced cardiovascular events in people with HIV.

**Public Health Infrastructure Relevance:** The trial explicitly leveraged NIAID's Clinical Research Sites (CRS) infrastructure, with infrastructure support provided by NIAID. The study also adapted to include COVID-19 assessment during the pandemic, demonstrating the flexibility of the clinical research infrastructure. Results were published in the New England Journal of Medicine (2023).

### 4.2 RISE Study — Improving Part C Early Intervention Service Delivery for Children with ASD

| Field | Details |
|---|---|
| **NCT ID** | NCT05114538 |
| **Official Title** | Improving the Part C Early Intervention Service Delivery System for Children With ASD: A Randomized Clinical Trial |
| **Status** | ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING |
| **Phase** | N/A |
| **Lead Sponsor** | Rush University Medical Center — OTHER |
| **Collaborators** | NIMH (NIH), Michigan State University, University of Massachusetts (Boston & Worcester), University of Washington, University of South Carolina |
| **Study Type** | Interventional — Randomized, Parallel, Single-Blind |
| **Primary Purpose** | Treatment |
| **Conditions** | Autism Spectrum Disorder |
| **Enrollment** | 440 (estimated) |
| **Start Date** | September 27, 2021 |
| **Primary Completion** | May 31, 2026 |
| **Estimated Completion** | May 31, 2027 |
| **Illinois Site** | Rush University Medical Center (Chicago) |

**Summary:** This study aims to increase timely and equitable access to autism-specialized early intervention by capitalizing on the existing infrastructure of the Part C Early Intervention (EI) system — a publicly funded service available in all U.S. states for children under age 3 with developmental delays. The study trains EI providers to deliver Caregiver-Implemented Reciprocal Imitation Teaching (CI-RIT), an evidence-based, parent-mediated intervention. It employs a hybrid type 1 effectiveness/implementation design across 20 EI agencies in 4 U.S. states, targeting 160 EI providers and 440 families.

**Public Health Infrastructure Relevance:** This trial directly addresses public health infrastructure by leveraging and strengthening an existing publicly funded service delivery system (Part C EI). The study examines how to scale evidence-based interventions within existing infrastructure, which has direct implications for how public health systems can be optimized without creating parallel systems. The implementation science framework (MADI) used provides a model for infrastructure improvement that could be applied across other public health domains.

**Public Health Infrastructure Relevance:** This trial is a direct example of public health infrastructure utilization and enhancement during a crisis. It demonstrates how existing community-academic partnership networks can be rapidly mobilized for pandemic response. The study's use of social network strategies for testing and vaccination outreach provides a scalable model for public health infrastructure that reaches the most disenfranchised populations — a critical equity consideration for bond-funded public health investments.

## 6. Summary of Trends and Implications

### Trial Status Trends
- **66.7% completed** — The research portfolio is mature, with most trials having generated published results that can inform policy decisions.
- **33.3% active** — One ongoing trial (RISE) will continue generating data through 2027, providing future evidence for early intervention service delivery.

### Trial Phase Trends
- The presence of one **Phase 3 trial** (REPRIEVE) demonstrates the region's capacity for large-scale, high-evidence clinical research.
- Two **N/A-phase trials** reflect the growing importance of behavioral and implementation science research, which uses different methodological frameworks than drug development trials. This is particularly relevant for public health infrastructure, where service delivery models rather than pharmaceuticals are the primary intervention.

### Sponsor Type Trends
- **NIH direct sponsorship** (1 trial) and **NIH collaborative funding** (in both "OTHER" sponsored trials) indicate strong federal investment.
- Academic institutions (Rush University, University of Chicago) demonstrate local leadership capacity, serving as lead sponsors for 2 of 3 trials.
- No industry-sponsored trials were identified for this search term, suggesting that public health infrastructure research is primarily a public-sector and academic endeavor.

### Investment Implications for Bond Oversight
1. **Infrastructure gaps in collar counties:** The absence of dedicated clinical trial sites in Lake, DuPage, Kane, Will, and McHenry counties suggests that bond-funded public health infrastructure investments could target these areas to expand research capacity.
2. **Academic medical center capacity:** Cook County's four major academic medical centers (Northwestern, Rush, UIC, University of Chicago) form the backbone of the region's clinical research infrastructure. Continued bond support for these institutions is critical.
3. **Community partnership models:** The C3 trial demonstrates that community-academic partnerships can effectively reach vulnerable populations. Bond-funded infrastructure should consider supporting community-based research sites, not just hospital-based sites.
4. **Implementation science:** The RISE study's focus on scaling evidence-based interventions within existing public health systems (Part C EI) provides a model for how bond-funded infrastructure improvements can be evaluated for effectiveness.

## 7. Limitations

1. **Search specificity:** The search used the term "public health infrastructure" which may not capture all relevant trials. Broader search terms (e.g., "public health," "community health," "health system") may identify additional trials.
2. **Location granularity:** The ClinicalTrials.gov API filters by state, not county. While all identified trials have Chicago (Cook County) sites, the full location data was manually reviewed to confirm county-level distribution.
3. **Collar county access:** While no dedicated trial sites exist in collar counties, residents of these counties may participate in Chicago-based trials. The absence of collar county sites does not necessarily mean collar county residents are excluded from research.
4. **Small sample size:** Only 3 trials were identified, limiting the statistical power of trend analyses. This small number reflects the specificity of the "public health infrastructure" search term.

---

## 8. Recommendations for the Committee

1. **Expand search scope:** Consider commissioning a broader analysis using additional search terms (e.g., "community health," "health equity," "preventive health") to capture a more comprehensive picture of regional clinical trial activity.
2. **Invest in collar county research infrastructure:** Bond proceeds could be allocated to establish or upgrade clinical research facilities in Lake, DuPage, Kane, Will, and McHenry counties, reducing geographic disparities in research access.
3. **Support community-based research:** Models like the C3 trial's social network strategy demonstrate that community-based research sites can effectively reach vulnerable populations. Bond-funded infrastructure should include community health centers and local health departments.
4. **Track ongoing trials:** The RISE study (NCT05114538) will complete in 2027. The committee should monitor its results, which will provide evidence on how to optimize publicly funded early intervention systems.
5. **Leverage NIH funding:** Two of three trials received NIH funding. Bond-funded infrastructure improvements that enhance the region's research capacity could attract additional federal research dollars.
